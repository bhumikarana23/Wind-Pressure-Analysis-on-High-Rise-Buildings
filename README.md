# Wind Pressure Analysis on High-Rise Buildings Under Extreme Wind Conditions

**Tools & Technologies:** AutoCAD | ANSYS Fluent | Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD)

## 1. Project Overview

This academic project focuses on investigating wind pressure distribution and airflow behaviour around high-rise buildings under extreme wind conditions.

The study explores the interaction between wind flow and building geometry using computational fluid dynamics (CFD), with the objective of understanding pressure variations across building surfaces and their implications for structural design.

The project combines building geometry development, numerical simulation and planned experimental validation to study wind-induced effects on high-rise structures.

## 2. Project Objectives

- Develop a representative high-rise building model for wind analysis.
- Investigate airflow patterns around the building under specified wind conditions.
- Analyse wind pressure distribution across different building surfaces.
- Understand the influence of building geometry on wind-induced pressure variations.
- Establish a basis for comparing numerical simulation results with experimental observations.

## 3. Methodology

### Phase 1: Building Geometry Development – AutoCAD

- Developed the high-rise building geometry using AutoCAD.
- Prepared the geometric model for subsequent computational analysis.
- Established the building configuration required for wind simulation.

### Phase 2: Computational Fluid Dynamics – ANSYS

- Set up the computational model for wind flow analysis.
- Performed CFD simulations in ANSYS under specified simulation conditions.
- Investigated airflow behaviour and pressure distribution around the building.
- Examined the numerical results to understand wind-induced pressure variations.

### Phase 3: Experimental Validation – Planned

- Experimental investigation is planned to study wind behaviour around the building model.
- Numerical simulation results will be compared with experimental observations.
- The comparison will help evaluate the reliability of the computational analysis.
